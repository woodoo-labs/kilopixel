Stroke

Every shape in Kilopixel supports responsive, customizable stroke rendering. Learn how to control line width, apply solid or gradient strokes, configure line caps and joins, and create animated dashed lines — all rendered crisply across any screen resolution.

Unitless Logical Width

Stroke attributes like strokewidth, linedash, and dashoffset operate directly in Kilopixel's 1000-width logical canvas. A strokewidth="4" is always exactly 0.4% of the stage width, automatically scaling across any display resolution with crisp clarity without blurring.

1. Stroke Color & Width

You can outline any Kilopixel shape using the stroke and strokewidth attributes:

  • Stroke Color: The stroke attribute accepts any valid CSS color (hex, RGB, HSL, or named colors) as well as gradient shaders. If omitted or set to none, no stroke is drawn.
  • Stroke Width: The strokewidth attribute sets the outline thickness in logical canvas coordinates (defaults to 1). Setting it to 0 disables stroke rendering.
<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer>
    <!-- Circle with configurable stroke -->
    <pxl-circle stroke="#f97316" strokewidth="40" r="150" x="300" y="300" fill="#ffedd566"></pxl-circle>

    <!-- Rounded rectangle with gradient stroke -->
    <pxl-rect stroke="#3b82f6" strokewidth="8" r="50" x="700" y="300" w="300" h="300" fill="#dbeafe66"></pxl-rect>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

2. Line Caps

The linecap attribute controls the ending shape of open paths — including <pxl-line>, open <pxl-polyline>, and open circular arcs (<pxl-circle> with sweep or start/end). Kilopixel supports three standard cap styles:

  • Butt: The default butt style cuts the stroke flush exactly at the path coordinates. The stroke ends strictly at the endpoints without extending past them.
  • Round: The round style adds a semicircular cap extending past each endpoint by half the stroke width (radius = strokewidth / 2).
  • Square: The square style adds a rectangular cap extending past each endpoint by half the stroke width.

Open Paths vs. Closed Shapes

linecap only affects paths with open endpoints. Closed shapes (such as <pxl-rect> or full 360° circles) close their subpaths automatically, so line caps are not rendered on them.

<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer>
    <!-- 1. Straight Line -->
    <pxl-line linecap="butt" strokewidth="40" rotate="0" x="300" y="300" x1="-150" y1="0" x2="150" y2="0" stroke="#f97316"></pxl-line>

    <!-- 2. Circular Arc -->
    <pxl-circle linecap="butt" strokewidth="40" sweep="270" r="150" start="135" x="700" y="300" stroke="#3b82f6" fill="none"></pxl-circle>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

3. Line Joins

The linejoin attribute determines how two connected stroke segments meet at a corner or vertex. Kilopixel supports three standard corner join styles:

  • Miter: The default miter style extends the outer edges of the stroke segments until they intersect in a sharp point.
  • Round: The round style rounds off the corner with a circular curve having a radius equal to half the stroke width.
  • Bevel: The bevel style slices off the outer corner with a flat diagonal edge between the outer endpoints of the two segments.

Open vs. Closed Corner Joins

Line joins apply to every vertex where two segments meet — including intermediate vertices of open <pxl-polyline> shapes, as well as the closing joint of closed shapes like <pxl-polyline closed="true"> and <pxl-rect>.

<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer>
    <!-- 1. Open Chevron Corner -->
    <pxl-polyline linejoin="miter" strokewidth="70" points="150,450; 300,150; 450,450" stroke="#f97316" fill="none"></pxl-polyline>

    <!-- 2. Closed Polygon Corner -->
    <pxl-polyline linejoin="round" strokewidth="40" points="550,450; 700,150; 850,450" closed="true" stroke="#3b82f6" fill="#dbeafe66"></pxl-polyline>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

4. Miter Limit

The miterlimit attribute establishes the maximum ratio between the outer point of a corner (miter length) and the stroke width. When two connected line segments join at a sharp acute angle, a miter join extends outward into a needle. To prevent runaway spikes from distorting the design, the engine clips the vertex into a flat bevel whenever the miter ratio exceeds this threshold.

  • Threshold Trigger: When the miter ratio exceeds miterlimit, the sharp point automatically slices into a flat bevel.
  • Default Limit (10): Allows sharp miters on almost all natural angles down to roughly 11.5° while preventing runaway spikes.

The Miter Ratio & Scale Invariance

The geometric miter length is strokewidth / sin(θ / 2). Because the ratio is computed by dividing this length by strokewidth, stroke width cancels out completely: ratio = 1 / sin(θ / 2). This makes clipping purely angle-dependent, ensuring sharp corners behave consistently regardless of stroke thickness or scale.

<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer>
    <!-- 1. Open Chevron Corner -->
    <pxl-polyline miterlimit="10" linejoin="miter" strokewidth="40" points="220,450; 300,150; 380,450" stroke="#f97316" fill="none"></pxl-polyline>

    <!-- 2. Closed Diamond Corner -->
    <pxl-polyline miterlimit="10" linejoin="miter" strokewidth="40" points="700,150; 762,300; 700,450; 638,300" closed="true" stroke="#3b82f6" fill="#dbeafe66"></pxl-polyline>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

5. Dashed Strokes & Offset

The linedash and dashoffset attributes allow you to create patterned, dashed outlines across any Kilopixel shape. Rather than drawing a continuous path, the stroke alternates between drawn dash segments and empty gaps.

  • Dash Pattern: The linedash attribute accepts a comma-separated list of numbers defining alternating dash and gap lengths (e.g., linedash="[24, 16]"), or a single number for equal dashes and gaps (e.g., linedash="24"). JavaScript array notation (e.g., [24, 16]) is also supported.
  • Phase Shift: The dashoffset attribute shifts where the pattern begins along the path perimeter, enabling precise alignment or manual sliding.
<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer>
    <!-- 1. Circle with Dash & Gap Spacing -->
    <pxl-circle x="300" y="300" r="140" stroke="#f97316" strokewidth="20" linecap="butt" fill="none"
      linedash="[24, 28]"
      dashoffset="0">
    </pxl-circle>

    <!-- 2. Polyline with Pattern Presets -->
    <pxl-polyline points="520,300; 640,180; 760,420; 880,300" smooth="1" stroke="#3b82f6" strokewidth="12" linecap="round" fill="none"
      linedash="[44, 28, 10, 28, 10, 28]"
      dashoffset="0">
    </pxl-polyline>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

6. Marching Ants & Continuous Animation

Combining linedash with a dynamic dashoffset expression produces continuous stroke animation — commonly known as marching ants. By multiplying the framework's time driver t by a velocity coefficient (e.g. dashoffset="-t * 40"), the dash pattern advances smoothly along the path perimeter at 60fps.

Directionality & Flow

A negative offset rate (-t * speed) advances the pattern forward along the path — moving clockwise around closed boundaries and from start to end along open paths. A positive offset rate (t * speed) reverses the flow direction.

<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer>
    <!-- 1. Selection Marquee (Closed Rect) -->
    <pxl-rect x="300" y="300" w="280" h="280"
      stroke="#f97316" strokewidth="4" fill="#ffedd522"
      linedash="[12, 12]"
      dashoffset="-t * 40">
    </pxl-rect>

    <!-- 2. Signal Conduit (Dormant Wire + Animated Pulse Runner) -->
    <pxl-polyline points="520,300; 640,180; 760,420; 880,300" smooth="1"
      stroke="#1e3a8a33" strokewidth="20" linecap="round" fill="none">
    </pxl-polyline>
    <pxl-polyline points="520,300; 640,180; 760,420; 880,300" smooth="1"
      stroke="#3b82f6" strokewidth="20" linecap="round" fill="none"
      linedash="[40, 80]"
      dashoffset="-t * 60">
    </pxl-polyline>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>
