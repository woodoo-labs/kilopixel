Conic Gradient

Kilopixel offers highly flexible conic (angular sweep) gradients for rotational color sweeps, radar effects, color wheels, and angular lighting.

To work with gradients in Kilopixel, you first need to understand two core concepts: the Bounding Box and the colorsArray.

Bounding Box: In Kilopixel, all gradients are mapped to an element's invisible bounding box. The top-left corner of any shape is always exactly (0, 0), and the bottom-right is (1, 1). This guarantees that if you move, scale, or stretch your shape, the gradient stretches seamlessly with it — no complex trigonometry required!

In the diagrams below, the blue coordinates represent the fixed corners of the shape's invisible 0-to-1 bounding box space.

colorsArray: All gradients in Kilopixel share a common parameter called colorsArray to define their colors. You can provide this array in two ways:

  • Colors Only: Pass an array of just colors (e.g., ['red', 'yellow', 'blue']). Kilopixel will automatically space them out evenly from 0 to 1 around the 360° circle.
  • Color Stops: Pass an array alternating between numeric offsets (0 to 1) and colors for precise control (e.g., [0, 'red', 0.5, 'yellow', 1, 'blue']).

1. Conic Gradient (1-Value Mode)

The 1-value mode is the simplest form of the conic gradient. In this mode, the origin is automatically locked to the shape's bounding box center (0.5, 0.5).

1-Value Mode Syntax

fill="conic(startAngle, colorsArray)"

The parameter startAngle defines the starting angle in degrees (0°–360°). The gradient sweeps clockwise around the center, where 0° points directly right along the +X axis (3 o'clock).

In this example, drag the Start Angle slider to rotate the gradient sweep around the center (0.5, 0.5). The purple arrow indicates the exact angle where the gradient begins (offset 0) and completes its 360° revolution.

<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer>
    <pxl-rect 
      x="500" y="300"
      w="400" h="250" 
      rotate="0"
      fill="conic(0, ['#e2e8f0', '#475569'])">
    </pxl-rect>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

2. Conic Gradient (3-Value Mode)

The 3-value mode allows you to position the center of rotation anywhere within (or beyond) the shape's bounding box by specifying Center [cx, cy] alongside the Start Angle.

3-Value Mode Syntax

fill="conic([startAngle, cx, cy], colorsArray)"

The parameters cx and cy define the origin using normalized bounding box coordinates (from 0 to 1). A center of [0.5, 0.5] is the geometric center of the shape, while [0, 0] is the top-left corner and [1, 1] is the bottom-right corner.

In this example, adjust the Center X0 and Center Y0 sliders to shift the center of rotation, and drag the Start Angle slider to orient the sweep. The purple dot marks the center of rotation, and the purple arrow indicates the starting ray.

<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer>
    <pxl-rect 
      x="500" y="300" 
      w="400" h="250" 
      rotate="0"
      fill="conic([45, 0.3, 0.3], ['#e2e8f0', '#475569'])">
    </pxl-rect>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>
