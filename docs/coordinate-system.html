The Coordinate System

Understanding Kilopixel's coordinate system is essential for creating complex animations without fighting the math. The engine handles transformations natively through a unique dual-coordinate approach and a responsive unit scaling system.

Pivot + Offset Architecture

Unlike traditional canvas contexts where you have to explicitly ctx.translate(), ctx.rotate(), and ctx.translate() back, Kilopixel natively splits position into Pivot and Offset.

1. The Pivot Point (x and y)

  • x and y define the absolute position of the element on the stage.
  • They act as the Center of Rotation (the pivot point).
  • When you apply rotate, scale, or skew, the transformation occurs perfectly around this point.

2. The Offset (dx and dy)

  • dx and dy define a local offset that is applied after rotation and scaling.
  • This allows an element to be pushed away from its pivot point.

Example: A Perfect Orbit

Creating an object orbiting a center point takes just one line of HTML, completely eliminating the need for trigonometry or nested groups!

<!-- A red circle orbiting at 60 degrees per second around the center -->
<pxl-circle 
  x="500" 
  y="500" 
  dx="200" 
  rotate="t * 60" 
  radius="20" 
  fill="red"
/>

Because rotate happens before the dx offset, the element revolves perfectly around the x/y pivot at a radius of 200.

Responsive Scaling Unit (u)

Kilopixel graphics are intrinsically responsive and scale to fit their container automatically. You don't have to write different math for mobile and desktop screens.

Logical Stage Width: Always 1000

Regardless of the actual pixel width of the <canvas> on the screen, the logical coordinate space of the stage is always based on a fixed width of 1000.

  • The horizontal center is always x="500".
  • The left edge is always x="0".
  • The right edge is always x="1000".

How u Works

Before spatial coordinates (x, y, dx, radius, stroke width, etc.) are passed to the canvas drawing API, the engine multiplies them by a dynamic scaling unit called u. u is simply clientWidth / 1000.

This unit makes your entire scene perfectly responsive while preserving crisp, DPI-aware rendering via ResizeObserver and device-pixel-content-box.

The Golden Rule: Use Raw Numbers

Because the width is strictly 1000, ALWAYS use raw numbers for horizontal coordinates.

Do NOT do this (Slow):

<pxl-circle x="ref.main.width / 2" y="500" />

Do THIS (Fast):

<pxl-circle x="500" y="500" />

By using raw numbers, you instantly hit the framework's "Fast Path" compiler, bypassing expensive proxy subscriptions and mathematical evaluation.
