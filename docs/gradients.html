Gradients

Kilopixel offers a highly flexible generative gradient system. Learn how to map linear, radial, and conic gradients accurately to your shapes, seamlessly animated and updated at 60fps.

To work with gradients in Kilopixel, you first need to understand two core concepts: the Bounding Box and the colorsArray.

Bounding Box: In Kilopixel, all gradients are mapped to an element's invisible bounding box. The top-left corner of any shape is always exactly (0, 0), and the bottom-right is (1, 1). This guarantees that if you move, scale, or stretch your shape, the gradient stretches seamlessly with it — no complex trigonometry required!

In the diagrams below, the blue coordinates represent the fixed corners of the shape's invisible 0-to-1 bounding box space.

colorsArray: All gradients in Kilopixel share a common parameter called colorsArray to define their colors. You can provide this array in two ways:

  • Colors Only: Pass an array of just colors (e.g., ['red', 'yellow', 'blue']). Kilopixel will automatically space them out evenly from 0.0 to 1.0.
  • Color Stops: Pass an array alternating between numeric offsets (0 to 1) and colors for precise control (e.g., [0, 'red', 0.8, 'blue']).

1. Linear Gradients (Angle Mode)

In Angle Mode, you provide a single number representing the angle in degrees (0 to 360). Kilopixel projects a geometrically perfect angle from the center of the shape and calculates the exact distance needed to cover the farthest corners.

This means the gradient is always drawn at a strict, perfect angle on the screen, even if the bounding box of the shape is a very wide rectangle.

Angle Mode Syntax: (True geometric degrees)

fill="linear(angle, colorsArray)"
<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer>
    <pxl-rect 
      x="500" y="300" 
      w="400" h="200" 
      rotate="0" 
      fill="linear(45, [0.4, '#e2e8f0', 0.6, '#94a3b8'])">
    </pxl-rect>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

Example 2: The Gallery

Because the angle projection is purely geometric, it works perfectly on every shape and doesn't distort when shapes are rotated or resized. Try adjusting the size and rotation of the shapes below to see how the gradients remain locked to their absolute angles.

<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer>
    <!-- 1. Ellipse -->
    <pxl-ellipse x="150" y="300" rx="100" ry="200" rotate="0" 
      fill="linear(45, ['orange', 'gold', 'tomato'])"></pxl-ellipse>
      
    <!-- 2. Hard Split Rounded Rect -->
    <pxl-rect x="500" y="300" w="200" h="200" r="20" rotate="0" 
      fill="linear(135, [0, '#3b82f6', 0.5, '#3b82f6', 0.501, '#93c5fd', 1, '#93c5fd'])"></pxl-rect>

    <!-- 3. Gradient Text -->
    <pxl-text x="850" y="300" size="150" rotate="90" text="'Pixel'" weight="800" align="center" baseline="middle"
      fill="linear(45, ['#c026d3', '#db2777', '#f43f5e'])"></pxl-text>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>
