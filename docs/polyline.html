Polyline

The polyline shape draws connected paths through a list of points. It supports smooth curves, closed and filled outlines, coordinates that animate one by one, and start and end arrowheads.

1. Points

A polyline connects a list of points with straight segments. Points are separated by semicolons and the x and y values of a point by a comma, so points="50,300; 150,200; 350,400; 450,300" draws a zig-zag through (50, 300), (150, 200), (350, 400) and (450, 300).

Every x and every y value is its own expression. A value can use math like min(a, b), follow a variable like ref.* or animate on its own like sin(t), while all other points stay where they are: points="50,300; 150,200 + sin(t) * 100; 350,400; 450,300" moves only the peak up and down.

<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer>
    <!-- Left: Zig-Zag -->
    <pxl-polyline stroke="#f97316"
      points="50,300; 150,200; 350,400; 450,300">
    </pxl-polyline>

    <!-- Right: Bow Tie -->
    <pxl-polyline stroke="#3b82f6"
      points="850,200; 950,300; 850,400; 650,200;
              550,300; 650,400; 850,200">
    </pxl-polyline>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

2. Smooth

smooth turns the straight segments into a curve that still passes through every point. A bare smooth is the same as smooth="1".

The number sets the tension: 0 keeps the segments straight, 1 is the default and higher values bend the curve further out. At 1 the zig-zag becomes a wave, at about 1.7 the bow tie becomes an infinity sign.

<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer>
    <!-- Left: Zig-Zag -->
    <pxl-polyline stroke="#f97316" smooth="1"
      points="50,300; 150,200; 350,400; 450,300">
    </pxl-polyline>

    <!-- Right: Bow Tie -->
    <pxl-polyline stroke="#3b82f6" smooth="1"
      points="850,200; 950,300; 850,400; 650,200;
              550,300; 650,400; 850,200">
    </pxl-polyline>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

3. Closed & Filled

A polyline stops at its last point. closed adds the segment from the last point back to the first one, so the outline wraps around. There is no need to repeat the first point: the bow tie below uses six points and closed instead of ending on 850,200 again.

fill paints the area inside the outline. The fill always reaches back to the first point, even on an open polyline: closed only decides whether that last edge is stroked. With smooth, a closed polyline becomes a smooth loop without a start or an end.

<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer>
    <!-- Left: Zig-Zag -->
    <pxl-polyline stroke="#f97316" fill="#ffedd566"
      points="50,300; 150,200; 350,400; 450,300"
      smooth="0" closed="false">
    </pxl-polyline>

    <!-- Right: Bow Tie -->
    <pxl-polyline stroke="#3b82f6" fill="#dbeafe66"
      points="850,200; 950,300; 850,400; 650,200; 550,300; 650,400"
      smooth="0" closed="true">
    </pxl-polyline>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

4. Sharp Corners

A smooth polyline bends at every point. To keep a sharp corner, repeat that point, like 250,450; 250,450: the curve arrives and leaves the point without rounding it, while all other points stay smooth. The heart repeats its bottom tip and the dip between its lobes.

A polyline that ends on its first point is smoothed across that point, like a closed one. The teardrop ends on 750,150 again, so its tip is round. Repeat that point once more and the tip turns sharp.

<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer>
    <!-- Left: Heart -->
    <pxl-polyline stroke="#f97316" fill="#ffedd566"
      points="250,450; 250,450; 50,250; 150,150;
              250,200; 250,200; 350,150; 450,250"
      smooth="1" closed="true">
    </pxl-polyline>

    <!-- Right: Teardrop -->
    <pxl-polyline stroke="#3b82f6" fill="#dbeafe66"
      points="750,150; 850,300; 800,400; 700,400;
              650,300; 750,150; 750,150"
      smooth="1">
    </pxl-polyline>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>
