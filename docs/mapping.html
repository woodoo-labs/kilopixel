Coordinate Mapping

The toLocal() function maps another shape's position, direction and size into the coordinate space of the shape that reads it. Shapes can follow, connect or copy shapes in other layers and groups, however these are moved, rotated or zoomed.

1. Target in a Transformed Layer

A shape inside a moved, rotated or zoomed layer keeps its x and y while the layer carries it across the stage. A plain reference such as ref.planet.x therefore returns the position inside the layer, not where the planet appears.

To reach the planet from outside its layer, use toLocal(ref.planet, 'x') and toLocal(ref.planet, 'y'). They return the planet's position in the space where the reading shape's own coordinates are read, so the result can be used directly as an attribute value. Here, the end point of a line follows the planet.

<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <!-- Planet, in a transformed layer -->
  <pxl-layer x="500" y="300" rotate="0" zoom="1">
    <pxl-circle id="planet" r="40" fill="#ffedd566" stroke="#f97316" strokewidth="4"
      x="200" y="100">
    </pxl-circle>
  </pxl-layer>

  <!-- Line, in an untransformed layer -->
  <pxl-layer>
    <pxl-line stroke="#22c55e" strokewidth="4"
      x1="150" y1="150"
      x2="toLocal(ref.planet, 'x')" y2="toLocal(ref.planet, 'y')">
    </pxl-line>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

2. Reader in a Transformed Layer

toLocal() returns values for the space in which the reading shape's own x and y are read: the space of its parent. The reading shape can therefore sit in a moved or rotated layer itself. Here, a ring in layer 2 surrounds the planet in layer 1.

The readout shows the returned values. They are coordinates of layer 2, not of the stage: when layer 2 moves or rotates, the values change and the ring stays on the planet.

<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <!-- Left: Layer 1, holds the planet -->
  <pxl-layer x="300" y="300" rotate="0">
    <pxl-circle id="planet" x="150" y="0" r="40" fill="#ffedd566" stroke="#f97316" strokewidth="4">
    </pxl-circle>
  </pxl-layer>

  <!-- Right: Layer 2, the ring follows the planet -->
  <pxl-layer x="700" y="300" rotate="30">
    <pxl-circle r="60" stroke="#3b82f6" strokewidth="4"
      x="toLocal(ref.planet, 'x')" y="toLocal(ref.planet, 'y')">
    </pxl-circle>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

3. Center, Offset and Total Position

A shape's x and y are its center (the pivot): rotate and zoom turn and scale the shape around this point. dx and dy then move the drawn shape away from the center. The planet below has dx="150", so it is drawn at a distance of 150 from its center, and its rotate moves it around the center like an orbit.

toLocal() can read both points. Its second argument chooses which one:

  • Center: toLocal(ref.planet, 'x') and toLocal(ref.planet, 'y') return the center. Use them for rings and orbits around the shape.
  • Offset: toLocal(ref.planet, 'dx') and toLocal(ref.planet, 'dy') return the offset, turned into the reading shape's space.
  • Total Position: toLocal(ref.planet, 'tx') and toLocal(ref.planet, 'ty') return where the shape is drawn, its total position (center + offset), as the readout shows. Use them for lines that connect to the shape.
<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <!-- Planet, drawn at its offset dx="150" -->
  <pxl-layer x="400" y="300" rotate="0">
    <pxl-circle id="planet" x="200" y="0" r="40" fill="#ffedd566" stroke="#f97316" strokewidth="4"
      dx="150" rotate="0">
    </pxl-circle>
  </pxl-layer>

  <!-- Readers, in an untransformed layer -->
  <pxl-layer>
    <!-- Ring, orbit around the center -->
    <pxl-circle r="ref.planet.dx" stroke="#3b82f6" strokewidth="4"
      x="toLocal(ref.planet, 'x')" y="toLocal(ref.planet, 'y')">
    </pxl-circle>

    <!-- Line, to the drawn planet -->
    <pxl-line stroke="#22c55e" strokewidth="4"
      x1="150" y1="520"
      x2="toLocal(ref.planet, 'tx')" y2="toLocal(ref.planet, 'ty')">
    </pxl-line>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

4. Rotation

A shape's rotation (rotate) turns it inside its parent. When the parent layer and group are rotated as well, the rotations add up: the pointer below points in the direction of both angles plus its own. ref.pointer.rotate still returns only the pointer's own value.

toLocal(ref.pointer, 'rotate') returns the pointer's total rotation in degrees, seen from the reading shape's space. The needle uses it as its own rotate, so it points the same way as the pointer, although it sits in another layer.

<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <!-- Left: Pointer, in a group in a rotated layer -->
  <pxl-layer x="250" y="300" rotate="0">
    <pxl-group x="150" y="-100" rotate="0">
      <pxl-line id="pointer" x2="100" stroke="#f97316" strokewidth="4" arrowend="auto"
        x="100" y="50" rotate="90">
      </pxl-line>
    </pxl-group>
  </pxl-layer>

  <!-- Right: Needle, copies the rotation -->
  <pxl-layer>
    <pxl-line x="850" y="150" x1="-50" x2="50" stroke="#3b82f6" strokewidth="4" arrowend="auto"
      rotate="toLocal(ref.pointer, 'rotate')">
    </pxl-line>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

5. Zoom

A shape's uniform scale (zoom) resizes it inside its parent. When the parent layer and group are zoomed as well, the factors multiply: the planet below is drawn 1.2 × 1.5 = 1.8 times its size. ref.planet.zoom still returns only the planet's own value.

toLocal(ref.planet, 'zoom') returns the planet's total zoom, seen from the reading shape's space. The ring multiplies the planet's radius by it and adds 15, so it surrounds the planet with the same gap at any zoom.

Stretched Shapes

When something along the way is stretched (scalex and scaley differ), a single zoom can't describe the size. 'zoom' then returns the average factor, and toLocal(ref.planet, 'scalex') and toLocal(ref.planet, 'scaley') return the width and height factors.

<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <!-- Planet, in a group in a zoomed layer -->
  <pxl-layer x="300" y="300" zoom="1.2">
    <pxl-group x="150" y="0" zoom="1.5">
      <pxl-circle id="planet" r="40" fill="#ffedd566" stroke="#f97316" strokewidth="4"
        x="100" y="0" zoom="1">
      </pxl-circle>
    </pxl-group>
  </pxl-layer>

  <!-- Ring, in an untransformed layer -->
  <pxl-layer>
    <pxl-circle stroke="#3b82f6" strokewidth="4"
      x="toLocal(ref.planet, 'x')" y="toLocal(ref.planet, 'y')"
      r="ref.planet.r * toLocal(ref.planet, 'zoom') + 15">
    </pxl-circle>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

6. Copying a Whole Frame

Rotation and zoom are enough as long as nothing along the way is stretched, skewed or mirrored. Otherwise the parts combine: below, the layer is stretched and the flag is turned inside it, so the flag leans, although no element sets a skew. To copy a target's frame exactly (its position, direction, lean and size), the reading shape maps six values, as the outline around the flag does:

  • Center: toLocal(ref.flag, 'x') and toLocal(ref.flag, 'y') return the flag's center, as in section 1.
  • Direction and Lean: toLocal(ref.flag, 'rotate') returns the direction of the flag's x axis, toLocal(ref.flag, 'skewx') the lean of its y axis.
  • Width and Height: toLocal(ref.flag, 'scalex') and toLocal(ref.flag, 'scaley') return the total scale along both axes. 'scaley' is negative when the flag is mirrored.

Six values describe any frame, so the outline needs no zoom and no skewy from the flag: 'scalex' and 'scaley' already contain the zoom, and a skew along y can be expressed with the other values. The outline's own zoom stays free: zoom="1.2" draws the copy 1.2 times as large around the flag's center.

If the target has an offset (dx, dy), map 'tx' and 'ty' instead of 'x' and 'y' and leave out the offset: the copy then sits on the drawn shape, and its own zoom grows it around the shape instead of around the distant center.

Zoom Multiplies the Scale

zoom multiplies both axes: a shape with zoom="2" and scalex="1.5" is drawn 3 times as wide and 2 times as tall. toLocal() reports these drawn factors, so 'scalex' returns 3 and 'scaley' returns 2, not the attribute values 1.5 and 1.

<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <!-- Flag, in a stretched layer -->
  <pxl-layer x="250" y="300" rotate="0" scalex="1.6">
    <pxl-polyline id="flag" stroke="#f97316" strokewidth="4" fill="#ffedd566" closed="true"
      points="-60,90; -60,-90; 90,-45; -30,0; -30,90"
      x="100" y="0" rotate="30" skewx="0" scaley="1">
    </pxl-polyline>
  </pxl-layer>

  <!-- Outline, copies the whole frame -->
  <pxl-layer>
    <pxl-polyline stroke="#3b82f6" strokewidth="4" closed="true" zoom="1.2"
      points="-60,90; -60,-90; 90,-45; -30,0; -30,90"
      x="toLocal(ref.flag, 'x')" y="toLocal(ref.flag, 'y')"
      rotate="toLocal(ref.flag, 'rotate')" skewx="toLocal(ref.flag, 'skewx')"
      scalex="toLocal(ref.flag, 'scalex')" scaley="toLocal(ref.flag, 'scaley')">
    </pxl-polyline>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

7. Draw Order

Layers are drawn in the order they appear in the markup, and the shapes inside a layer as well. A value that changes over time (t, wave()) is evaluated when its shape is drawn. A shape that reads such a target before the target is drawn gets the value from the previous frame and trails one frame behind.

Put reading shapes after their targets: in a later layer, or later in the same layer. Below, both planets orbit at the same speed. On the left, the ring's layer comes before the planet's layer and the ring lags behind; on the right, it comes after and the ring stays on the planet. The faster the orbit, the larger the gap.

Slider Changes Don't Lag

Values set from outside (a slider, setAttribute(), a <pxl-var> that a slider changes) update every reading shape right away, in any order. Only values that change over time depend on the order. An animated <pxl-var> belongs before everything that reads it, for example at the top of the first layer that uses it.

<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <!-- Left: Ring First, the planet covers it -->
  <pxl-layer>
    <pxl-circle r="45" stroke="#3b82f6" strokewidth="4"
      x="toLocal(ref.leftPlanet, 'x')" y="toLocal(ref.leftPlanet, 'y')">
    </pxl-circle>
  </pxl-layer>
  <pxl-layer x="250" y="330" rotate="t * 360">
    <pxl-circle id="leftPlanet" x="170" r="30" fill="#ffedd566" stroke="#f97316" strokewidth="4">
    </pxl-circle>
  </pxl-layer>

  <!-- Right: Ring Last, it covers the planet -->
  <pxl-layer x="750" y="330" rotate="t * 360">
    <pxl-circle id="rightPlanet" x="170" r="30" fill="#ffedd566" stroke="#f97316" strokewidth="4">
    </pxl-circle>
  </pxl-layer>
  <pxl-layer>
    <pxl-circle r="45" stroke="#3b82f6" strokewidth="4"
      x="toLocal(ref.rightPlanet, 'x')" y="toLocal(ref.rightPlanet, 'y')">
    </pxl-circle>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>
