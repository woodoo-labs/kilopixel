Coordinate System

Understanding the coordinate system is the key to mastering Kilopixel. Unlike standard Canvas APIs which use physical pixels, Kilopixel uses a responsive, logic-based coordinate space designed for declarative markup.

The 1000px Logical Canvas — Yes, this is literally why it's named Kilopixel.

To make graphics fully responsive without any media queries, every Kilopixel <pxl-stage> has a fixed logical width of 1000 units, regardless of the physical screen size.

This means an x-coordinate of 500 is always perfectly in the horizontal center of the canvas. The engine handles all the complex scaling math behind the scenes.

<pxl-stage ratio="16 / 9">
  <pxl-layer>
    <pxl-grid stroke="#f1f5f9" major="2" labels="true"></pxl-grid>
    
    <!-- Perfectly centered horizontally at x=500 -->
    <pxl-circle x="500" y="280" r="50" fill="#3b82f6"></pxl-circle>
    
    <!-- A red arrow pointing to the 500x mark -->
    <pxl-line 
      x1="50" y1="280" 
      x2="430" y2="280" 
      stroke="#f43f5e" 
      strokewidth="4" 
      arrowend="auto">
    </pxl-line>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

Pivot Points (x & y)

In standard HTML/CSS, x and y usually refer to the top-left corner of an element. In Kilopixel, x and y always define the absolute center and pivot point of the shape.

When you apply a rotation (e.g., rotate="t * 45"), the shape rotates cleanly around this exact pivot point.

<pxl-stage ratio="16 / 9">
  <!-- We center the entire layer to simplify the math -->
  <pxl-layer x="500" y="280">
    <pxl-grid stroke="#f1f5f9" major="2" labels="true"></pxl-grid>
    
    <!-- Rotating Square -->
    <pxl-rect x="0" y="0" w="100" h="100" fill="#3b82f6" rotate="t * 45"></pxl-rect>
    
    <!-- Red dot marking the pivot point at 0,0 -->
    <pxl-circle x="0" y="0" r="6" fill="#f43f5e"></pxl-circle>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

Local Offsets (dx & dy)

This is where Kilopixel's coordinate system shines. The dx and dy attributes apply a local offset after rotation and scaling.

By keeping the pivot point (x/y) fixed at the center, but offsetting the shape by dx="150", you can create perfect orbital mechanics mathematically effortlessly!

<pxl-stage ratio="16 / 9">
  <pxl-layer x="500" y="280">
    <pxl-grid stroke="#f1f5f9" major="2" labels="true"></pxl-grid>
    
    <!-- The Sun (Pivot Point) -->
    <pxl-circle x="0" y="0" r="30" fill="#f59e0b"></pxl-circle>

    <!-- Orbiting Planet -->
    <!-- It rotates around 0,0 but is pushed away by dx=150 -->
    <pxl-circle 
      x="0" y="0" 
      dx="150" 
      r="20" 
      fill="#3b82f6" 
      rotate="t * 90">
    </pxl-circle>
    
    <!-- Red Arrow illustrating the dx offset vector -->
    <pxl-line 
      x="0" y="0" 
      x1="45" y1="0" x2="115" y2="0" 
      stroke="#f43f5e" strokewidth="4" arrowend="auto" 
      rotate="t * 90">
    </pxl-line>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>
