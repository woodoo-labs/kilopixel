Coordinate System

Understanding the coordinate system is the key to mastering Kilopixel. Unlike standard Canvas APIs which use physical pixels, Kilopixel uses a responsive, logic-based coordinate space designed for declarative markup.

1. The 1000-Unit Logical Canvas

To make graphics fully responsive without any media queries, every Kilopixel <pxl-stage> has a fixed logical width of 1000 units. The height is dynamically calculated based on the ratio attribute.

For a ratio="5 / 3" stage, the height is 600. This means a shape at x="500" y="300" is always perfectly centered, regardless of the physical screen size.

<!-- Placing a shape exactly in the center of the canvas -->
<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer>
    <pxl-circle x="500" y="300" r="200" stroke="#f97316"></pxl-circle>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

2. Relative Coordinates (Layer Translation)

Working in global coordinates can quickly become difficult to manage. Instead, you can translate the entire <pxl-layer> to create a local coordinate space. This is a fundamental concept for building complex, movable scenes:

  • Global Positioning: The layer itself is positioned at x="500" y="300", placing its origin at the exact center of the global 1000-unit stage.
  • Local Origin: Because the shape lives inside the layer, the layer's center point acts as the local (0, 0) origin for the shape.
  • Independent Movement: If you drag the Layer sliders to move it, notice that the shape's x and y coordinates do not change. The shape physically moves across the screen because its parent layer is moving, but its local mathematical position remains strictly relative to the layer's origin.
<!-- Centering the layer instead of the shape -->
<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer x="500" y="300">
    <pxl-circle x="150" y="200" r="300" stroke="#f97316"></pxl-circle>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

3. Rotation

In Kilopixel, applying rotation is incredibly simple. When you rotate a shape, it spins around its x and y center point by default.

However, it is important to understand the difference between Layer and Shape rotation:

  • Layer Rotation: Rotating a <pxl-layer> spins the entire coordinate space inside that layer around the layer's center point (x, y). The shape's relative coordinates do not change, but the local space it lives in spins together with the layer.
  • Shape Rotation: Rotating a shape (such as a <pxl-ellipse>) only spins that specific shape around its own x and y center point, leaving the rest of the layer untouched.
<!-- An ellipse spinning perfectly around its center point -->
<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer x="500" y="300" rotate="0">
    <pxl-ellipse 
      x="200" y="150" 
      rx="250" ry="100" 
      rotate="20" stroke="#f97316">
    </pxl-ellipse>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

4. Local Offsets (dx, dy)

This is where Kilopixel's coordinate system becomes incredibly powerful. The dx and dy attributes offset the shape from its center x and y pivot point.

Because this offset happens inside the shape's local coordinate system, any rotation applied will cause the shape to orbit the pivot point instead of just spinning in place.

<!-- A rectangle orbiting its center point -->
<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer x="200" y="150" rotate="0">
    <pxl-rect 
      x="300" y="150" 
      dx="150" dy="150" 
      rotate="0" 
      w="100" h="100" stroke="#f97316">
    </pxl-rect>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

5. Element Referencing (ref.*)

Any element with an id is automatically registered in Kilopixel's zero-GC pxl.nodes registry. Other elements can declaratively bind to its properties at 60fps using the ref.* prefix.

In this example, three shapes exist inside the same coordinate system. Both Follower B and Follower C lock their origin coordinates (x and y) directly to ref.leaderA.x and ref.leaderA.y, while using independent dx and dy attributes to define their relative offset from Leader A!

<!-- Followers binding their origin to a Leader shape -->
<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer x="500" y="300" rotate="0">
    <!-- Leader A Shape (Anchor Origin) -->
    <pxl-circle id="leaderA" x="0" y="150" r="12" fill="#fb923c"></pxl-circle>

    <!-- Follower B (Blue Palette) -->
    <pxl-circle id="followerB"
      x="ref.leaderA.x" y="ref.leaderA.y"
      dx="-400" dy="-300"
      r="10" fill="#60a5fa">
    </pxl-circle>

    <!-- Follower C (Green Palette) -->
    <pxl-circle id="followerC"
      x="ref.leaderA.x" y="ref.leaderA.y"
      dx="150" dy="-350"
      r="10" fill="#4ade80">
    </pxl-circle>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

6. Coordinate Mapping (toLocal)

When shapes orbit or rotate inside nested coordinate spaces, drawing connecting lines or tracking positions requires translating between coordinate systems:

  • Why raw attributes stay constant: If you reference ref.pointA.x or ref.pointA.dx directly, you will always get their static declared values even while the point is rotating. Because the local coordinate system rotates with the shape, its internal x and dx numbers never change.
  • How toLocal solves this: By wrapping the reference in toLocal(ref.pointA, 'x') or toLocal(ref.pointA, 'y'), Kilopixel automatically calculates the shape's true orbiting position and maps it into your current coordinate space—no manual trigonometry required!

In this "constellation" example, two shapes orbit their centers independently. The connecting line uses toLocal to track their live positions and stay perfectly connected at 60fps.

<!-- A line dynamically connecting independent points -->
<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer x="500" y="300" rotate="0">
    <!-- Orbiting Points -->
    <pxl-circle id="pointA" x="-400" y="0" dx="0" dy="-200" rotate="0" r="5" fill="#1d4ed8"></pxl-circle>
    <pxl-circle id="pointB" x="150" y="0" dx="0" dy="200" rotate="0" r="5" fill="#c2410c"></pxl-circle>

    <!-- Connecting Line -->
    <pxl-line 
      x1="toLocal(ref.pointA, 'x')" y1="toLocal(ref.pointA, 'y')" 
      x2="toLocal(ref.pointB, 'x')" y2="toLocal(ref.pointB, 'y')" 
      stroke="#94a3b8" strokewidth="2">
    </pxl-line>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>

7. Animated Orbits

This is where Kilopixel's coordinate system shines. By combining local offsets with dynamic rotation, we can create perfect orbital mechanics mathematically effortlessly!

The dx attribute pushes the planet away from the Sun (the x and y pivot point), and the rotate attribute spins that entire offset vector around the center.

Use the sliders below to see how standard HTML inputs can instantly drive the Kilopixel canvas via the ref.* registry.

<!-- Standard HTML Inputs driving Kilopixel variables -->
<input type="range" oninput="pxl.nodes.ex7OrbitOffset.set('value', this.value)">
<input type="range" oninput="pxl.nodes.ex7OrbitSpeed.set('value', this.value)">

<pxl-stage ratio="5 / 3">
  <pxl-layer x="500" y="300">
    <!-- Kilopixel Variables -->
    <pxl-var id="ex7OrbitOffset" value="150"></pxl-var>
    <pxl-var id="ex7OrbitSpeed" value="90"></pxl-var>

    <!-- The Sun (Pivot Point) -->
    <pxl-circle r="30" fill="#f59e0b"></pxl-circle>

    <!-- Orbiting Planet -->
    <pxl-circle 
      dx="ref.ex7OrbitOffset.value" 
      rotate="t * ref.ex7OrbitSpeed.value" 
      r="20" fill="#3b82f6">
    </pxl-circle>
  </pxl-layer>
</pxl-stage>
